About me

I use geospatial thinking to understand how cities work for people.

Researcher, educator, and engineer working between Taiwan and Indonesia.

Hello, I am Febrian Fitryanik Susanta, an Assistant Professor in Geodetic Engineering at Universitas Gadjah Mada and a PhD candidate in Geomatics at National Cheng Kung University.

My research sits at the intersection of geospatial science, transportation, and human-centered urban planning. I study pedestrian safety, walkability, public-transport accessibility, and route choice—especially how age and the built environment shape people’s experience of moving through a city.

Across my academic and professional work, I have contributed to spatial-data infrastructure, municipal geoportals, 3D cadastre, disaster visualization, asset monitoring, and WebGIS projects. These experiences have taught me to connect rigorous spatial methods with tools that planners, institutions, and communities can actually use.

As an educator, I teach GIS, geospatial analysis, and spatial-web technologies while supervising student research and project-based learning. I enjoy collaborations that bring together maps, data, and public purpose to make mobility and essential services safer and more equitable.

Research focus

Where maps meet human experience.

My work connects spatial analysis, transportation, and urban accessibility. I combine GIS, network science, and interpretable machine learning to reveal patterns—and translate them into decisions people can act on.

01 / Mobility

Pedestrian safety & walkability

Understanding how the built environment shapes crash risk, route choice, and walking conditions across age groups.

02 / Access

Spatial accessibility

Measuring access to hospitals, public transport, and essential services through network-based and time-sensitive models.

03 / Systems

Geospatial platforms

Designing WebGIS, geoportals, and visual analytics that turn complex spatial data into useful public infrastructure.
